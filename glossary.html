How Texas Hold'em Works

Texas Hold'em is the most popular variant of poker. Here's the flow of a single hand:

  1. Blinds are posted. Two players to the left of the dealer post forced bets: the small blind and big blind. This creates a starting pot.
  2. Hole cards are dealt. Each player receives 2 private cards face-down. Only you can see these.
  3. Pre-flop betting. Starting left of the big blind, each player can fold, call, or raise.
  4. The Flop. Three community cards are dealt face-up. Another round of betting follows.
  5. The Turn. A fourth community card is revealed. Another betting round.
  6. The River. The fifth and final community card is dealt. Final betting round.
  7. Showdown. Remaining players reveal their hands. Best 5-card combination wins the pot.
Game flow

The four streets

Pre-Flop

You have only your 2 hole cards. Decide to play or fold based on hand strength and position.

Flop

3 community cards are revealed. See how your hand connects — pairs, draws, or nothing.

Turn

The 4th card arrives. Pot grows, draws either hit or miss. Stakes increase.

River

The final card. All cards are on the table. Last chance to bet, bluff, or fold.

Beginner tips

10 things to remember

01

Play tight early

As a beginner, play fewer hands but play them aggressively. Fold weak hands without remorse.

02

Position is power

Acting last gives you more information. Prefer playing hands when you're on the button or in late position.

03

Don't chase every draw

Having four cards to a flush is exciting, but calling big bets to chase it is often a losing play.

04

Watch your opponents

Even online, notice betting patterns. Who always raises? Who only bets big with strong hands?

05

Manage your bankroll

Never risk money you can't afford to lose. A good rule: have 20–30 buy-ins for your stake level.

06

Understand pot odds

If the pot is ₹1000 and you need to call ₹200, you're getting 5:1. Your draw should hit more than 1 in 6 times.

07

Fold more often

Beginners play too many hands. It's okay to fold 70–80% of hands dealt to you.

08

Bet with purpose

Every bet should have a reason: for value (you think you're ahead) or as a bluff (to make opponents fold).

09

Don't bluff too much

Beginners over-bluff. Against players who call everything, bluffing is just giving away money.

10

Review your hands

After playing, think about key decisions. "Would I do the same thing again?" is the best learning question.
